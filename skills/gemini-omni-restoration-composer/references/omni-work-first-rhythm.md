# Work-First Construction Rhythm

Read this for ordinary construction VIDEO prompts. It governs worker boundaries, material logistics and rhythm; it does not change the profile's shot count, anchor layout, construction dependencies, or threshold/reward behavior.

## Boundary Instants, Not Arrival And Departure Scenes

Keep both IMAGE anchors person-free. The first video instant matches the starting anchor; immediately afterwards the worker reaches or leans in from just off-frame into first effective tool contact, or takes one short step when access requires it. Entry is part of the first working action, never its own shot.

Choose a physically reachable work face. Do not stretch arms across a room, put a worker on an unprovided elevated platform, or teleport a body into the image. If the existing anchors require a longer approach, use the shortest credible approach within the working shot; do not change locked framing or fake reachability to meet an arbitrary timing target.

The last effective action includes withdrawing all visible hands/body fully out of frame. If the torso is visible, withdrawing only the hands is insufficient. Match the ending anchor for the final instant without a separate walk-out, packing-up sequence or empty hold. Keep the worker's identity and physical weight throughout.

## Material Continuity Without Repeated Logistics

A source may be a stack, container or support already present in the starting anchor, or a brief edge-of-frame handoff directly into installation. Show its location, contact, movement and consumption without requiring a delivery scene every time. Do not invent pre-staged stock absent from a locked starting anchor.

Waste may be captured in a nearby crate, chute or growing pile as part of removal. State a plausible destination and volume; a whole panel cannot vanish as a handful of dust. Retain installed materials, remaining stock, resting tools and access equipment when the next anchor retains them. Only items declared absent from that anchor need visible removal. Never clear the whole site merely because the clip ends.

## One Milestone, Operation-Specific Evidence

Spend the clip on one meaningful state change and its physical evidence, not extra tasks to fill time. Choose emphasis from the actual operation:

- Demolition: separation, resistance and debris capture.
- Installation: alignment, fixing and the structure becoming legible.
- Coating: an advancing coverage edge, roller/brush contact and surface texture.
- Finishing: the final fit or correction and an unobstructed result.

These are choices, not another mandatory sequence. Compare adjacent beats and vary the first action, evidence and final gesture, not just synonyms. Do not impose a universal second-by-second arrival/work/exit schedule.

Follow the existing shot structure. Inserts prove this operation's contact or traces rather than generic tool beauty shots. They do not advance completion. Resume at the same completion level and show remaining work; only explicitly stated same-way repetitions of already-demonstrated actions may be compressed. A cut cannot create unperformed construction.

## Review

Reject contradictory people in person-free anchor instants, unexplained material disappearance, or physically impossible access. Strengthen clips that spend separate shots on arrival, unloading, departure or empty holds, or repeat identical action choreography across adjacent beats. A short edge reach/withdrawal is compliant; retained material is not a cleanup failure.

Apply these rules through the beat's concrete actions. Do not append this entire policy to every delivered prompt or exceed its word budget.
